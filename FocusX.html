What the program does

The webcam frame goes to a face landmark model that returns 478 points, five of them on each iris. FocusX turns the iris position inside the eye socket into a screen coordinate, moves a cursor there, and fires a click when your gaze rests on one spot. That single pipeline drives the keypad, the pointer and the attention meter.

How the gaze is mapped to the screen

For each eye FocusX measures where the iris sits between the inner and outer corner, and between the upper and lower lid. Those two ratios, plus the head position, become eight features. Calibration collects samples at nine known points and solves a least squares system for the weights that turn those features into pixels, which also cancels out the mirror flip and your seating distance.

How the focus score is built

  • Gaze steadiness: low scatter over the last three seconds scores high, wandering scores low.
  • Blink rate: 8–25 blinks a minute is the comfortable band; far above or below pulls the score down.
  • Eyes closed fraction: long closures read as drowsiness and cut the score sharply.
  • Head motion and time spent looking off screen round it off.

Running it for your submission

  1. Save the page as focusx.html and open it in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Sit about 50–70 cm from the screen with light on your face, not behind you.
  3. Start tracking, allow the camera, then calibrate all nine points.
  4. Keep “Mouse demo” ready as a backup so the viva never depends on the lighting in the room.

Everything runs inside the browser. No frame ever leaves the machine and nothing is uploaded.
